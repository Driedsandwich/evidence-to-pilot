**Why work together (hypothesis):** Proposal: test transfer of a rule-based engine that reads Gerber files and a netlist, extracts geometric emission-risk features (trace-to-plane spacing, clock routing, decoupling placement, via stubs) and outputs a score plus violation list. Hypothesis: these rules may flag Nexara's 150–450 MHz hotspots and give engineers a self-run pre-sign-off check. Transfer is unproven: the tool was validated on other boards against CISPR 22 Class B, shows a 22% false-positive rate, and is described as an early gate, so Nexara's five boards must test generalisation.

**Research question:** Joint-project topic: can layout-scoring rules locate the 150–450 MHz hotspots of Nexara's five archived boards before testing? Conditional post-pilot industrial PhD question, only if go: which calibrated rule set predicts emissions on new boards?

**Three-month pilot:** Baseline: Nexara's existing scans and lab outcomes. Weeks 1–12: score all five boards blind to results using a threshold fixed in advance, compare flagged violations with scan hotspots. Add HyperLynx emission checks only if kickoff verification shows the licences support them; otherwise drop.

**Decision gate:** Kickoff pre-check: scans must give peak frequencies or locations mappable to layout features; failure is no-go for hotspot comparison. Coincidence: flagged feature within 20 mm of a hotspot or at a harmonic inside a scan peak band. Go: threshold not tuned on these boards; at least 2 of 3 failed boards flagged; at most 1 of 2 passed boards flagged; at most 10 unmatched violations per board. Negotiable.
